What that means, in plain language

A frontier lab is training a model to be good at cybersecurity. You cannot teach that with a textbook — you teach it by making the model attempt real security problems thousands of times and rewarding it when it solves them. Each problem is a self-contained environment: a broken or compromised system, a goal, and an automated test that scores the attempt. That test is the training signal. We supply 100 such environments, all in the supply-chain-attack space, each labelled with the exact weakness it teaches (its CWE) and its risk category (its OWASP class).

The three things every task must be

01

Real & industrial

A genuine engineering scenario — a tampered dependency, a poisoned build. No toy problems, no LeetCode, no exam questions. It must pass human review.

02

Provably hard

Measured by how often strong models fail it. If today's best models solve it easily, it teaches nothing — it must sit at the edge of their ability.

03

Un-cheatable

The model must not be able to shortcut the answer. A test it can game trains the model to cheat — the fastest way to get a task rejected.

The acceptance gate — what "hard enough" means

Difficulty is graded on mean reward (average score across repeated attempts). All four conditions must hold at once. Hy = Hy4-Preview in think mode; SOTA = Opus 5 / GPT-5.6-sol.

IDConditionThreshold
D1Hy4-Preview is mostly failing it (≥8 attempts)mean ≤ 0.4

D4SOTA isn't failing every single timevariance ≠ 0

Plus a length gate: the task must take a model many steps — Hy4-Preview > 80 turns, or Opus5 > 60 turns. That's what makes it "long-horizon," not a quick puzzle.

Why this suits us — and where the line is

The scarce skill is security, not AI horsepower

We don't train the model. We build the gym and the referee. Knowing what a real CWE-506 tamper looks like, and writing a test that can't be gamed, is a security skill — exactly SilentMesh's edge. Our own model being weak is irrelevant to our half of the work.

What we don't hold is the rollout infrastructure — running Hy4-Preview and Opus 5 to produce those mean-reward numbers. That is Pazago's existing infrastructure, and the clean seam of the joint venture.
